I think it's useful to have **two schedules**, not one:

1. **Deep Learning Mode** – when you have 2 hours and can use your books.
2. **Maintenance Mode** – when life is busy and you only have podcasts/audio.

This way you never have to decide _whether_ to study—only _which schedule_ to follow.

---

# Your Priority Order

If you have to cut something, keep them in this order:

1. 🥇 Pimsleur
2. 🥈 Assimil
3. 🥉 Anki
4. Grammar Progressive
5. Vocabulary Progressive
6. TV5MONDE / RFI
7. Coffee Break French
8. Paul Noble

The reasoning is that **speaking and listening skills fade faster than grammar knowledge**, so on busy weeks it's better to keep your ears and mouth active than to worry about completing another unit in a textbook.

# My Final Recommendation (The "Non-Negotiables")

No matter how busy life gets, try to do these:

### ✅ Ideal Day (2 hours)

- 20 min Anki
- 30 min Grammar
- 20 min Vocabulary
- 30 min Assimil
- 20 min ChatGPT
- 30 min Pimsleur (during commute or exercise)

### ✅ Busy Day (30–45 minutes)

- 30 min Pimsleur
- 10–15 min Anki (if possible)

### ✅ Extremely Busy Day (10–20 minutes)

- Listen to Coffee Break French **or** replay a completed Pimsleur lesson while commuting or walking.
- Mentally describe your surroundings or your day in simple French for a few minutes.

This approach keeps you moving forward without feeling that you've "fallen off the wagon." Over the 18–24 months you're planning for, that consistency will matter far more than having a perfect study week every week.
