### Books

**Picture books / early readers**

- **T’choupi** (Thierry Courtin) — toddler staple
- **Petit Ours Brun**
- **La Reine des couleurs**, **Loup** series (Orianne Lallemand) — hugely popular preschool/early elementary series
- **Petit Ours Brun**, **Trotro** (the little donkey) — classic toddler characters

**Elementary/middle-grade**

- **Le Petit Nicolas** (Goscinny/Sempé) — near-universal in French childhoods
- **Les Contes de Perrault** and **La Fontaine’s Fables** — classic cultural bedrock, often memorized in school
- **Le Petit Prince** — read in schools, though more literary than a typical “kid’s book”
- **Roald Dahl** in French translation (Charlie et la Chocolaterie, Matilda) — widely read even though originally English

**Comics/BD (huge in French-speaking culture — bigger than in English-speaking countries)**

- **Astérix**
- **Tintin**
- **Lucky Luke**
- **Gaston Lagaffe**
- **Titeuf** — very popular with actual kids/tweens, more contemporary slang-heavy French
- **Boule et Bill**

### Movies/TV

- **T’choupi et doudou**, **Petit Ours Brun** — toddler shows
- **Oui-Oui** (Noddy) — preschool
- **Franklin**, **Caillou** — actually Canadian-produced (Franklin is Canadian!), which is a nice bonus for you given your immigration goal — these often use more neutral/Québécois-adjacent French
- **Une vie de chat**, French animated films generally
- **Les Triplettes de Belleville**, **Kirikou et la sorcière**, **Ernest et Célestine** — beloved French animated films, good for slightly older kids and genuinely enjoyable as an adult learner
- Disney/Pixar films dubbed in French — useful because you likely know the plots already, so it’s easier to follow structurally

### Canada/Québec-specific angle (worth prioritizing, given your goal)

Since you’re immigrating to Canada, it’s worth deliberately including Québécois content, not just France-French:

- **Passe-Partout** — iconic Québécois children’s show, generational touchstone the way Sesame Street is in English Canada/US
- **Cornemuse**, **Bibi et Geneviève** — other Québécois kids’ shows
- **Franklin** (as above) — Canadian-made, French-dubbed version widely watched
- Radio-Canada’s kids’ programming (Radio-Canada Jeunesse) — worth browsing for current shows

### Why this matters beyond “cute content”

These aren’t just nostalgia — Astérix references, Petit Nicolas expressions, and fable morals from La Fontaine show up constantly in adult French conversation, journalism, and even exam listening passages as cultural shorthand. Getting exposed to them now means you’ll recognize allusions later that would otherwise sail past you, even at a high language level.
