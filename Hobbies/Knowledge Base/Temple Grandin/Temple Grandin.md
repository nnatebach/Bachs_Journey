The scientific term for Temple Grandin's specific ability is **object visualization** (or **object-visual thinking**). [[1](https://bluegemsaba.com/thinking-in-pictures-what-that-feels-like/), [2](https://www.multipleintelligencesoasis.org/blog/2022/11/17/temple-grandin-and-the-theory-of-multiple-intelligences)]

Cognitive scientists and psychologists divide [[visual thinker]]s into two main scientific categories:

- **Object Visualizers (Object-Visual Thinkers)**: This is the exact scientific classification for Grandin's mind. People with this cognitive style process information by creating highly detailed, photorealistic mental images. They treat thoughts holistically as pictures, movies, or 3D objects, allowing them to visually inspect mechanical blueprints or animal environments in their minds before anything is physically built. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking_in_Pictures), [2](https://bluegemsaba.com/thinking-in-pictures-what-that-feels-like/), [3](https://science.fas.harvard.edu/event/harvard-science-book-talk-october-12-temple-grandin), [4](https://www.multipleintelligencesoasis.org/blog/2022/11/17/temple-grandin-and-the-theory-of-multiple-intelligences)]
- **Spatial Visualizers (Visual-Spatial Thinkers)**: Unlike object visualizers who see detailed pictures, spatial visualizers think in abstract patterns, dimensions, geometry, and relationships. This cognitive style is more common among mathematicians, computer programmers, and statisticians. [[1](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/visual-thinking-by-temple-grandin/), [2](https://science.fas.harvard.edu/event/harvard-science-book-talk-october-12-temple-grandin), [3](https://www.multipleintelligencesoasis.org/blog/2022/11/17/temple-grandin-and-the-theory-of-multiple-intelligences), [4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEBasWOpkNI&t=70)]

In the broader context of psychology and neuroscience, this style of processing is referred to as __[[visuospatial coding]]__ or a **visual-spatial learning style**. In her pioneering book, [_Thinking in Pictures_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking_in_Pictures), Grandin described it simply by stating that words are like a "second language" to her, while highly detailed mental graphics are her primary tool for navigating the world.
