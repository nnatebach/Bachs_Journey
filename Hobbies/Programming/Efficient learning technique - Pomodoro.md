Plan it this way:

- **Session 1:** 60 min, then 15 min break
- **Session 2:** 60 min, then 15 min break
- **Session 3:** 60 min, then **30 min break** (this replaces the 15, not an addition to it)
- **Session 4 (optional):** treat it as a bonus, ideally lighter work like review or an easy project

By the end of session 3 you've done three hours of focused work, which is already near the upper limit for most people. Don't feel obliged to do a fourth.

A few tweaks:

1. **Treat your hour as a ceiling, not a target.** If an hour is the longest you can go _without_ getting frustrated or tired, plan on 50 to 55 minutes so you stop while you're still engaged. Ending a session slightly early is better than ending it burnt out, and you'll find it easier to start the next one.
2. **Use a mid-session check at about 30 minutes.** Take 30 seconds to ask whether you're making progress or just spinning. If you're stuck, switch approach or ask for help right then, rather than burning the other half of the hour.
3. **Use the long break for something real.** A meal, a walk, or exercise works better than another screen. It's the one that actually resets your focus for later in the day.
4. **Adjust to the material.** For really dense new concepts, shorter sessions (30 to 40 minutes) may serve you better, even if you can sit for an hour. Save the full hour for building projects or practice, where you can get into flow.

> [!TIP]
> If you're consistently fading around minute 45, shorten the sessions rather than forcing it.
