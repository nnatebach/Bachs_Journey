****1. Pause and predict.**** When the instructor puts a type on screen, stop the video and guess what the error or result will be, then press play. This takes no extra time and works better than writing notes.

****2. Code along in the TypeScript Playground.**** Go to [typescriptlang.org/play.](https://typescriptlang.org/play.) It runs in the browser with nothing to install. Retype the instructor's examples, then change one thing and see what breaks. About 10 minutes of this per lesson is more useful than an hour of passive watching.

****3. Apply each lesson to the real repo straight away.**** After Type Alias and Interface extends, open `ts/interfaces` and `ts/types` and find an example. After Union Types, look for one in the code. Seeing a lesson in the real project makes it stick, and it's the exact skill you need for the project.

****4. Write one sentence per lesson.**** Not notes, just: "A union type means ___." If you can't finish the sentence, rewatch that part.

****5. Test yourself afterwards.**** I can give you a short quiz on these six lessons, or you can paste any type from the repo and I'll walk through what it means. Being quizzed is one of the fastest ways to find what didn't sink in.
