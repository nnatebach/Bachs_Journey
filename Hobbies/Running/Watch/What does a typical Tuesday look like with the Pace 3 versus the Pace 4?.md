A typical Tuesday highlights the stark contrast between the two watches. Because sports watches are worn 24/7, the differences shape your entire day—from rolling out of bed to tracking a hard evening run.

Here is how a standard Tuesday plays out on your wrist with each device.

## 7:00 AM – Waking Up & Checking Readiness

- **Pace 3:** You wake up and glance at your wrist. The reflective Memory-in-Pixel (MIP) display relies entirely on ambient morning light. It looks completely passive—almost like an e-ink e-reader or a digital clock. If your bedroom is dark, you have to flick your wrist to trigger the backlight, which glows a soft, dim blue. You scroll the digital crown to check your overnight HRV (Heart Rate Variability) and sleep score.
    
- **Pace 4:** You wake up and your wrist movement triggers a sharp, vibrant AMOLED display. The screen looks crisp, contrast-heavy, and premium right out of bed. The 390x390 resolution makes the small text of your morning sleep metrics significantly easier to read without squinting.
    

## 12:30 PM – Midday Casual Wear & Notifications

- **Pace 3:** You are working at your desk or walking outside in blazing midday sunlight. The hotter and brighter the sun shines, **the clearer the Pace 3 becomes.** Because the MIP screen reflects external light, you can read text messages and calendar alerts perfectly at an angle without lifting your arm.
    
- **Pace 4:** Indoors, the AMOLED screen shines, looking incredibly modern. However, if you are wearing the stock silicone strap, this is around the time of day you might experience a common Reddit annoyance: the new secure button-tab on the tail of the strap flicks loose against your desk or sleeve, requiring you to tuck it back in. When notifications come through, you notice that while the screen is gorgeous, the notification text itself is still quite small.
    

## 6:00 PM – The Track Workout / Evening Run

This is where the user experience completely diverges. Imagine you are running a hard interval session or a fast tempo run as the sun goes down.

### Data Visibility Mid-Stride

- **Pace 3:** As dusk turns to dark, the screen gets harder to see. To check your current lap pace, you rely on the "Auto-Backlight" to kick in when you lift your arm, or you keep the backlight turned on permanently during the workout. The data fields are flat, but because the screen is always "on," a simple downward glance at your arm tells you your exact split instantly.
    
- **Pace 4:** The 1,500-nit AMOLED screen is an absolute powerhouse in low light. The data fields are bright, color-coded by heart rate zone, and incredibly sharp. However, if you turned off the Always-On Display (AOD) to save battery, you experience a fraction-of-a-second delay when you lift your arm mid-stride for the screen to wake up.
    

### Navigation & Map Adjustments

- **Pace 3:** If you need to check your breadcrumb route map to see an upcoming turn, you have to press the back button and fumble with scrolling the digital crown while running at a hard pace—which is highly irritating with sweaty hands.
    
- **Pace 4:** You hit the dedicated physical **Action Button** on the lower left. With one single click, you instantly jump straight to your breadcrumb navigation map. You check your turn, hit the Action Button again, and you are immediately thrown back to your exact data screen.
    

### Post-Workout Notes

- **Pace 3:** You hit save. The watch uploads to your phone via Bluetooth. You open the COROS app on your phone to type in how the run felt.
    
- **Pace 4:** You finish your intervals, hit save, and use the watch's built-in microphone to record a quick voice note: _"Felt strong on intervals 1-4, winded on 5."_ The watch automatically transcribes this into your training log on the app.
    

## 10:00 PM – Winding Down & The Battery Check

- **Pace 3:** You head to bed. You check your battery: **84%**. It has been at 84% for a while, and you won’t need to look for a charging cable for another week and a half. It completely disappears on your wrist because it is a mere 30 grams of featherlight plastic.
    
- **Pace 4:** You get ready for sleep. If you spent the day running the screen in Always-On Mode, you notice your battery dropped by about **15–20%** today alone. If you want to avoid charging anxiety by the weekend, you switch the watch into its dimmed "Sleep Display Mode" so the screen doesn't light up the bedroom every time you roll over.
