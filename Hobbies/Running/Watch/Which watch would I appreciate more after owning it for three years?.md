If you plan to strap a watch to your wrist and wear it every single day for the next three years, you will almost certainly appreciate the **COROS Pace 4** more.

Here is the straightforward, unsentimental breakdown of why, looking past the initial marketing hype.

### 1. The Software Wall (The Deciding Factor)

COROS is notorious for aggressive lifecycle management. They build great software updates, but they gate them behind newer processor architectures once the older hardware hits a structural wall.

- **The Pace 3 over 3 years:** It is already an aging platform. In three years, the Pace 3 will be functionally obsolete from a software standpoint. It will still track your runs perfectly, but it will have stopped receiving new firmware features long ago. It will feel like an abandoned legacy device.
    
- **The Pace 4 over 3 years:** Because it runs on COROS's newer chip architecture, it is guaranteed to sit right in the sweet spot of their development lifecycle. Over the next three years, you will get meaningful feature updates, metric refinements, and app integrations that the Pace 3 physically cannot support.
    

### 2. Physical Durability & Wear

Three years of sweat, showers, accidental wall-scuffs, and daily wear take a massive toll on lightweight sports watches.

- **The Pace 3:** It uses a performance polymer (plastic) lens. Over 36 months, unless you use a plastic screen protector, that face _will_ accumulate a spiderweb of micro-scratches. The plastic casing also tends to show smooth, polished wear spots over time.
    
- **The Pace 4:** It steps up to a mineral glass screen and a significantly denser, more polished outer casing. Three years down the line, a glass face simply holds up against casual scratches and look considerably cleaner than aged plastic.
    

### 3. Battery Degradation Reality

Lithium-ion batteries inevitably degrade. After three years of constant charge cycles, you can expect a typical smartwatch battery capacity to drop by roughly 15% to 20%.

- **The Battery Math:** The Pace 4 has a higher baseline battery capacity, specifically designed to handle the power-hungry AMOLED screen and dual-frequency GPS. Even if your daily battery drops slightly over three years, its raw performance tracking battery life (up to 41 hours of GPS) leaves a massive buffer. You won't be left with a watch that dies mid-marathon three years from now.
    

### The Verdict

The **Pace 3** is a fantastic short-term budget buy if you want to save money _right now_ and only care about fundamental data.

But if the goal is a **three-year commitment**, buying the Pace 3 means investing in a device that is already nearing the end of its evolutionary life. The **Pace 4** will feel like a supported, modern, and physically intact tool by year three, making it the clear winner for longevity.
