Six months after the release of the **COROS Pace 4**, the initial hype has settled on Reddit (`r/Coros`). Real-world users who upgraded or chose between the two watches have highlighted specific, long-term daily experiences.

The six-month consensus breaks down into distinct praises, complaints, and quirks from active community threads.

### 1. The Screen Debate: AMOLED vs. MIP

The shift from the Pace 3’s Memory-in-Pixel (MIP) display to the Pace 4’s AMOLED screen remains the most heavily debated topic.

- **The Praise (Pace 4):** Users with older eyes or those who frequently run in overcast or dark conditions heavily praise the change. One user (`u/BXO511`) noted: _"The AMOLED screen is way clearer for my eyes. The tiny text on the bottom data fields of the Pace 3 was hard to read, but the Pace 4 makes it easy."_
    
- **The Complaint (Pace 4):** Traditionalists strongly miss the "always-on" passive visibility of the Pace 3. Long-term owners complain about the minor lag of the wrist-wake gesture. As `u/elijahdesign` put it: _"I still prefer the MIP screen on the Pace 3 for quick glances without relying on a gesture. Coros added a hard-to-see Always-On Display mode just to match battery expectations."_
    

### 2. Battery Life Reality Check

The battery gap becomes very evident after half a year of logging workouts.

- **Pace 3 Consistency:** Users praise the Pace 3 because it consistently hits **14–15 days** of real-world use without thought.
    
- **Pace 4 Trade-offs:** If you leave the Always-On Display (AOD) active on the Pace 4, the battery drops significantly. Reddit users note it yields only **4–5 days** of charge with AOD on. For athletes who hate frequent charging, this has driven a few to stick with or buy a used Pace 3.
    

### 3. Early Batch Quality Control & Elevation Bugs

Six months in, several threads emerged regarding specific hardware and data stability bugs on the Pace 4.

- **The Complaint:** A subset of early adopters (`u/WorthSugar9235` and others) reported highly frustrating data drops: _"GPS accuracy dropped mid-run, and the elevation readings are jagged and 'saw-toothed'."_
    
- **The Resolution:** While users noted that COROS customer service was exceptionally fast at issuing warranty replacements for these defective units, it remains a notable stain on the Pace 4's early launch cycle compared to the rock-solid hardware stability of the older Pace 3.
    

### 4. The Physical Design & Daily Quirks

Long-term physical wear revealed subtle design flaws and wins.

- **The Strap Annoyance (Pace 4):** The new secure button/tab mechanism on the Pace 4's silicone strap is a frequent point of frustration. Multiple users complained that the excess strap loop constantly pops loose or flicks open several times a day during casual wear.
    
- **Premium Feel:** Conversely, users note that the Pace 4 looks and feels significantly less like a "cheap plastic toy" than the Pace 3. The casing feels more polished and holds up better to scratches over six months of daily abuse.
    

### 5. Future-Proofing vs. Discontinuation

A turning point in community sentiment occurred following news regarding the lifecycle of the older model.

- **The Reality:** With COROS signaling the wind-down of the Pace 3 to focus on the newer chip architecture (shared by the Pace 4 and Apex 4), Redditors heavily lean toward advising new buyers to spend the extra $60–$70 on the Pace 4. The consensus is that the Pace 4 is guaranteed to receive feature rollouts and firmware updates for the next 4–5 years, whereas the Pace 3 has reached its functional ceiling.
