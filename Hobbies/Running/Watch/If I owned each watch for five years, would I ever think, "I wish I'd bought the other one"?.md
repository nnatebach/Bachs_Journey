Over a five-year timeline, the reality of lithium-ion battery degradation and changing tech standards guarantees that you would eventually experience "the grass is greener" regret with either choice.

Here is exactly when and why you would wish you had bought the other watch by Year 5.

### If you chose the Pace 3, in Year 5 you will think:

> _"I wish I had bought the Pace 4."_

Your regret will hit around **Year 3 or 4**, and it will stem from **software abandonment**.

- **The Broken Ecosystem:** COROS aggressively freezes software feature rollouts on older processor architectures when they hit a physical memory or processing ceiling. By Year 5, your Pace 3 will have been completely cut off from new app features, performance metrics, and system updates for years. It will feel like a frozen piece of legacy tech.
    
- **The Scratched, Dim Lens:** The Pace 3 uses a plastic lens. After five years of being brushed against jacket zippers, bumped into walls, and rubbed against gym gear, that screen _will_ be heavily scratched and look weathered. Combined with the low-resolution 240x240 display, it will look incredibly dated compared to everything else on the market.
    
- **The Battery Fade:** When your battery naturally degrades by 20% over 5 years, the Pace 3's GPS tracking window shrinks significantly. Because the Pace 4 started with a much larger battery capacity optimized for intensive tracking, a degraded Pace 4 would still outlast your degraded Pace 3 on long weekend efforts.
    

### If you chose the Pace 4, in Year 5 you will think:

> _"I wish I had bought the Pace 3."_

Your regret will stem from **battery degradation colliding with an AMOLED screen**.

- **The 2-Day Smartwatch Reality:** Right out of the box, turning on the Always-On Display (AOD) on the Pace 4 drops the battery from 19 days down to 6 days. Fast forward five years: your lithium-ion battery has naturally degraded. Now, if you keep AOD on, your watch is dying every 2 to 3 days. You will deeply envy the old Pace 3 owners whose passive MIP screens don't draw power to display the time, allowing their 5-year-old watches to easily coast through a full week on a single charge.
    
- **The Broken Charging Connection:** COROS uses a plug-in charging port rather than a magnetic puck. Over 5 years of plugging and unplugging that cable, the internal port pins loosen. Combined with a degraded battery that requires frequent charging, trying to get the cable to sit _perfectly_ so it actually charges without disconnecting if you bump the table will become an incredibly frustrating daily chore.
    
- **Screen Burn-In Anxiety:** Leaving an AMOLED display on the same watch face with identical data fields for thousands of hours over five years introduces a statistical risk of ghosting or permanent screen burn-in—a hardware issue that simply does not exist on the Pace 3's MIP screen.
    

### The Verdict

- If you hate **obsolete software and a scratched-up, cheap-feeling casing**, you will regret the **Pace 3**.
    
- If you hate **frequent charging and low battery thresholds** down the line, you will regret the **Pace 4**.
    

If you want a device that remains fully supported and physically intact by the end of the decade, the **Pace 4** handles the five-year test better—as long as you accept that you'll have to turn off the Always-On Display in the later years to save the aging battery.
