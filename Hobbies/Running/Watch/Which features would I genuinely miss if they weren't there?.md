If you had to downgrade to a basic, entry-level watch tomorrow, there are three features across these two devices that you would immediately and painfully miss in your day-to-day training:

### 1. Dual-Frequency GPS (Both Pace 3 & Pace 4)

If you have ever used an older or cheaper watch that only connects to standard single-band GPS, you know the frustration of looking at your post-run map and seeing your route wildly zigzagging through buildings, clipping corners, or completely losing track under heavy tree canopy.

Both the Pace 3 and Pace 4 use **All-Satellite Dual-Frequency (L1+L5) tracking**. This is the feature you would miss most. It locks onto satellites in seconds and ensures that your current pace readings don't randomly spike or drop when you pass high-rise buildings or dense foliage. Your distance metrics are exact, meaning your training splits are actually accurate.

### 2. The COROS EvoLab Software Integration (Both Watches)

Because COROS doesn't paywall its training metrics behind premium subscriptions, the **EvoLab platform** is something you take for granted until it's gone.

- You would instantly miss the automatic **Effort Pace** metric (which translates your uphill/downhill data into what your pace _would_ be on a flat road).
    
- You would miss the clean tracking of your **Training Load, Base Fitness, and Fatigue trends** that tell you exactly when you are overtraining or ready to push harder.
    
- Seamless, automatic syncing with third-party apps like Strava or training plan platforms (like Runna) happens instantly in the background without you ever needing to manually export files.
    

### 3. The Hardware Quality-of-Life Upgrades (Pace 4 Only)

If you opt for the Pace 4, there are two distinct physical additions that you would heavily miss if you had to go back to the Pace 3:

- **The Dedicated Action Button:** Having a physical shortcut to instantly flip to your breadcrumb navigation map or alternate screens mid-workout—without having to meticulously scroll a tiny digital dial with sweaty fingers—becomes second nature very quickly.
    
- **The High-Resolution Contrast:** If you frequently run early in the morning, late at night, or on dark, overcast days, going back to a low-resolution, dim screen where the tiny data text at the bottom is fuzzy and requires a manual backlight press would feel like a massive step backward.
