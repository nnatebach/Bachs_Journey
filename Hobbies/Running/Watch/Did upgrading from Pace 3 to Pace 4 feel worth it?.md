For most people who already own a perfectly functioning **Pace 3**, upgrading to the **Pace 4** does **not feel worth the extra money**.

While the Pace 4 is an objectively better watch on paper, the community consensus shows that the day-to-day training experience feels remarkably similar. The decision boils down to whether you are genuinely limited by the Pace 3's hardware, or if you simply want a shinier screen.

Here is how the value judgment breaks down based on long-term user feedback:

### Why it feels like a waste to upgrade

- **The software is identical:** COROS uses the exact same EvoLab training metrics across all their watches. Upgrading your hardware won't change your post-run data, sleep tracking, or structured workout features.
    
- **The "AMOLED tax" on daily battery:** If you are used to the 15-day "charge it and forget it" lifestyle of the Pace 3, migrating to the Pace 4 with the Always-On Display active means you will be charging it every 5 days. For many, that feels like a downgrade in convenience.
    
- **The core running metrics are unchanged:** Both watches feature dual-frequency GPS. Unless you got an early-batch unit with an elevation bug, the actual distance and pace tracking on your weekend runs will look identical.
    

### When the upgrade actually feels worth it

The upgrade only feels like a justified jump if you fall into one of three specific camps:

1. **You are a nighttime or indoor runner:** If you regularly struggle to read the dim, low-contrast MIP screen of the Pace 3 during early morning, evening, or treadmill sessions, the 1,500-nit AMOLED screen on the Pace 4 feels like a massive quality-of-life upgrade.
    
2. **You rely heavily on mid-run breadcrumb maps:** The addition of the physical **Action Button** on the Pace 4 allows you to jump straight into your navigation maps and back to your data screens with one click. On the Pace 3, toggling maps requires fumbling with the digital crown mid-stride—which is an absolute chore when wearing gloves or sweating heavily.
    
3. **Your Pace 3 battery is degrading:** If you’ve owned your Pace 3 since launch and the battery life is starting to tank naturally, jumping to the Pace 4 makes sense because it features a much more robust battery architecture during active GPS tracking.
    

### The Bottom Line

If your Pace 3 works perfectly and you don't mind the old-school screen, **keep it.**

The Pace 4 is the definitive choice for _new_ buyers stepping into the COROS ecosystem because it is more future-proof, but as a pure upgrade for existing Pace 3 owners, it offers too many diminishing returns for the price.
