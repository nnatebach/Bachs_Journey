Yes, there is a very vocal contingent of runners on Reddit (`r/Coros`) who have explicitly chosen to stick with their **Pace 3**, and in some cases, have even bought a _second backup_ Pace 3 after the Pace 4 dropped.

The community members choosing to keep the Pace 3 fall firmly into a few distinct categories:

### 1. The "MIP Forever" Traditionalists

The absolute biggest reason users reject the Pace 4 and stay with the Pace 3 is the screen technology. For many pure outdoor runners, an AMOLED screen is an active downgrade.

- **The Sentiment:** Users highly prize the passive, reflective Memory-in-Pixel (MIP) display. As one user (`u/Apart-Ad9039`) put it: _"MIP forever. Have to start searching for old-new products. Had my Pace 3 since release day. I just prefer MIP."_
    
- **The Glancing Advantage:** Runners hate having to use an aggressive arm-flick or "wrist-wake gesture" mid-stride just to check their split times or heart rate. With the Pace 3, you can just glance down at your wrist under blazing direct sunlight, and the data is perfectly legible instantly.
    

### 2. The Battery Conscious

The Pace 3 remains highly valued by athletes who despise the charging cycles associated with modern smartwatches.

- **The Sentiment:** To match the Pace 3’s 15-day daily battery life, a Pace 4 user has to disable the Always-On Display (AOD). If you turn the Pace 4's AOD on, the battery drains to roughly 5 days. For those who want true "set-it-and-forget-it" convenience, the Pace 3 wins. Redditors note that the Pace 4 requires too many software workarounds—like a heavily dimmed "sleep display mode" and an overly faint daytime AOD—just to manage battery drain.
    

### 3. Early Batch Defect Avoidance

Because the Pace 3 has been on the market for years, its hardware and sensor manufacturing processes are mature and incredibly stable.

- **The Sentiment:** Following the launch of the Pace 4, several threads emerged regarding broken barometers, "saw-tooth" jagged elevation data tracking, and mid-run GPS drops on early-batch Pace 4 units. While COROS customer support is generally quick to replace defective watches, many users holding onto the Pace 3 noted that they didn't want to play "hardware roulette" with a first-generation production run when their current watch was already tracking flawlessly.
    

### 4. The Weight Factor

- **The Sentiment:** At just 30g with a nylon strap, the Pace 3 is lighter than the Pace 4. For ultrarunners, marathoners, and people who wear their watch to bed for sleep tracking, that slight difference matters. Users frequently comment that the Pace 3 "completely disappears" on your wrist, whereas the Pace 4 feels slightly more substantial and premium, which isn't always what a minimalist runner wants.
    

### Summary of Community Advice

If you check current threads, the overall advice is clear:

> "If you already own a Pace 3 that holds a strong charge, do not buy the Pace 4."

The only catch to keeping the Pace 3 long-term is future-proofing. COROS has begun winding down production of the Pace 3 to shift focus entirely to newer internal processor architectures. While the Pace 3 will continue to work perfectly for years, it has officially hit its structural ceiling for major new feature updates.
