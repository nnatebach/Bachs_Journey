Long-term feedback for the **COROS Pace 3** and the **COROS Pace 4** centers around a fundamental shift in hardware philosophy: the transition from an always-on, low-power MIP (Memory-in-Pixel) screen to a bright, modern AMOLED display.

The real-world, long-term praises and complaints for both watches highlight these differences.

### COROS Pace 3

The Pace 3 is widely regarded as a classic, no-nonsense "pure running tool." Because it uses a reflective MIP display, it appeals heavily to traditionalists.

#### Long-Term Praises:

- **The MIP Display (Legibility):** Users praise the screen for being perfectly legible in direct sunlight. Because it doesn’t rely on a backlight during the day, the time and your data fields are always completely visible at a glance without needing a "wrist-raise" gesture.
    
- **Exceptional Daily Battery Life:** In daily smartwatch mode, the Pace 3 consistently lasts around 15 days. Users love that they can ignore the charger for weeks at a time.
    
- **Extremely Lightweight and "Invisible":** At just 30 grams (with a nylon band), long-term owners consistently note that the watch completely disappears on the wrist. It is highly favored for sleep tracking and long endurance sessions because of its minimal footprint.
    
- **Unbeatable Budget Value:** Following price cuts after the Pace 4 launch, it remains highly praised as the best value-for-money pure GPS training watch on the market.
    

#### Long-Term Complaints:

- **Dim in Low Light/Indoor Conditions:** On overcast days, indoors, or during night runs, the screen can look dull, muddy, and difficult to read without manually activating the backlight.
    
- **Small Fonts & Low Resolution:** The 240x240 resolution means text and data fields look blocky. Users with older eyes or those trying to read 4–6 data fields simultaneously during a hard run complain that the text is too small and fuzzy.
    
- **Fragile Screen Lens:** The Pace 3 uses a plastic screen lens that long-term users note is prone to picking up micro-scratches over months of daily wear unless a screen protector is used.
    

### COROS Pace 4

The Pace 4 brings hardware updates, including an upgraded optical heart rate sensor, an extra physical button, a microphone for voice logs, and a premium glass lens. However, the experience is defined by its 1,500-nit AMOLED screen.

#### Long-Term Praises:

- **Vibrant, High-Resolution Screen:** The jump to a 390x390 AMOLED display is the most praised feature. It is incredibly sharp, crisp, and easy to read in dark or cloudy conditions. The upgraded mineral glass face also looks and feels significantly more premium than the Pace 3.
    
- **Superb GPS Battery Life:** Surprisingly, despite the power-hungry screen, the Pace 4 features an upgraded battery architecture that actually _beats_ the Pace 3 during active tracking. It handles dual-band GPS heavily without tanking the battery, making it a favorite for marathoners.
    
- **The "Action Button":** The addition of a third physical button (on the bottom left) is highly praised. Users love using it as a dedicated shortcut to jump straight to breadcrumb navigation maps and back to data screens mid-workout without fumbling with the digital crown.
    
- **Voice Features:** The built-in microphone allows for voice-based map pins and quick training log notes (like recording RPE/effort levels immediately after a workout), which multi-sport athletes find highly convenient.
    

#### Long-Term Complaints:

- **Sacrificed Daily Battery (with Always-On Display):** While gesture-wake mode nets about 19 days, turning on the Always-On Display (AOD) drops battery life down to just 5 to 6 days. Long-term users migrating from older sports watches find it frustrating to charge their watch weekly instead of bi-weekly.
    
- **The Gesture-Wake Latency:** For those who disable AOD to save battery, there is a minor but persistent complaint about the fraction-of-a-second lag when raising the wrist to wake the screen mid-stride.
    
- **The Silicone Strap Design:** A common physical complaint specific to the Pace 4's new silicone band is that the secure tab/button mechanism at the end tends to flick off or undo itself multiple times a day during casual wear.
    
- **Accidental Crown/Touch Triggers:** Because the Pace 4 is slightly larger (43.4mm vs 41.9mm) and sits flush, some users report accidentally scrolling the digital crown or triggering the touchscreen when wearing tight jacket cuffs or thick gloves.
    

### Summary: Which one wins out in user sentiment?

- **Choose the Pace 3 if:** You want an old-school, ultra-lightweight training tool where the screen is always visible in daylight, you hate charging your devices, and you want to save money.
    
- **Choose the Pace 4 if:** You run frequently at night or indoors, want a sharp and premium-looking display that is easy to read, value an extra physical button for navigation shortcuts, and don't mind charging your watch once a week.
