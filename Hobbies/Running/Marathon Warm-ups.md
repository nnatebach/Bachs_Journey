## Are they different for marathon and ultra (long distance) marathon?
Yes, they should be somewhat different, mainly because of pacing, distance, and what you're asking your body to do in the first few miles.

**Marathon warm-up**
A road marathon usually starts near or close to race pace fairly early, and the race is short enough (relatively speaking) that you don't want to burn matches before the gun goes off. So the warm-up tends to be:

- Shorter and more purposeful — maybe 10-15 minutes
- Dynamic stretches (leg swings, walking lunges, high knees) to get the joints moving
- A few short strides or accelerations (4-6 x 20-30 seconds) to prime the neuromuscular system and rehearse race pace
- Light jogging to raise heart rate and body temperature

The goal is to arrive at the start line already "switched on," since you'll likely settle into goal pace within the first mile or two.

**Ultra warm-up**
Ultras are a different game — you're not hitting anything close to race intensity early on, effort is much lower relative to threshold, and the race itself often serves as its own extended warm-up. So the approach shifts:

- Much lighter and shorter, sometimes almost minimal — a bit of walking, light dynamic mobility, maybe a short easy jog
- Less emphasis on strides or explosive priming, since you don't want elevated heart rate or fatigue before a race that could last many hours
- More focus on joint mobility and injury-prevention movements (ankles, hips, glutes) since you'll be on your feet for a long time, often on trails
- Some ultra runners barely warm up at all and just start conservatively, letting the first few miles serve as the ramp-up

**The underlying logic**

- Marathon: warm-up prepares you to hit near-threshold pace almost immediately
- Ultra: warm-up prepares your joints and mind for a long day, but conserves energy since the "real" warm-up happens naturally over the opening miles at an easy effort

One more practical difference: ultras often start early morning or involve long waits at the start (trail race logistics, shuttles, etc.), so many ultra runners plan their warm-up around not standing around too long after they've loosened up, and around weather/terrain (cold starts, technical trail footing) rather than around hitting pace.

### Full marathon warm-up
Total time: ~18-22 minutes
**1. Easy jog — 5-8 minutes**
Very light effort, just enough to raise core temp and get blood flowing. Should feel almost too easy.

**2. Dynamic mobility — 5-6 minutes**

- Leg swings (front-back and side-side) — 10 each leg
- Walking lunges — 10 steps
- High knees — 20 meters
- Butt kicks — 20 meters
- Walking quad pulls / hip openers — 10 each side

**3. Activation drills — 2-3 minutes**

- A-skips — 15-20 meters
- B-skips — 15-20 meters  
    _(Skip these if space is tight or you're not familiar with the movement — high knees/butt kicks above cover the basics fine.)_
- Glute bridges or bodyweight squats — 10-15 reps

**4. Strides — 4-6 x 20-30 seconds**  
Progressive accelerations up to just above goal race pace, full recovery (walk/easy jog) between each. This is the non-negotiable, most important piece — don't skip this even if you cut everything else short.

**5. Final settle — 2-3 minutes**  
Light walking, sip water, head to corral. Legs should feel "switched on" but heart rate settled.

**Bare-minimum version** (if you're short on time or space):  
Easy jog (5 min) → dynamic mobility (3-4 min) → strides (4x20-30 sec) → walk to corral.

**One adjustment for conditions:** if it's cold or you've got a long corral wait, extend the mobility/walking portion and add a layer, so you don't stiffen back up before the gun goes off.

That's the full routine — solid, race-tested, and flexible enough to trim if you're tight on time.
